How lenders see it

Lenders look at how much you earn, how consistently you earn it and the records that support your income. Tax returns and Notices of Assessment, often covering the past two years, are a common starting point.

The calculation varies by mortgage program. Some allow eligible business expenses to be added back to your income. Others use business bank statements or financial statements to help assess earnings. Your credit, existing debts and down payment or home equity also shape your options.

Which lender fits?
  • A
    A lenders: Banks and credit unionsOften assess income using tax returns and Notices of Assessment. Some programs allow adjustments for eligible business expenses.
  • B
    B lenders: Alternative lendersSome programs use business bank statements or financial statements to assess income. Down-payment requirements, fees and conditions vary.
  • C
    C lenders: Private lendersGenerally provide short-term financing, with greater emphasis on the property’s equity. The total cost and a realistic repayment plan are central considerations.

Questions people ask

Can I get a mortgage in my first two years of self-employment?

Some mortgage programs consider applicants with less than two years in business. Relevant work experience, training, taking over an established business or evidence of ongoing earnings may support an application. The lender and any mortgage insurer involved each have requirements you must meet.

Do lenders use my income before or after expenses?

Income after business expenses is a common starting point. Some mortgage programs allow eligible expenses to be added back when calculating qualifying income. Others assess earnings using business bank statements and supporting records. The approach depends on your business structure and the program.

Does it matter if I am incorporated?

Yes. A lender may review your company’s finances alongside your personal income documents to understand how the business supports your earnings. Corporate tax returns, financial statements and proof of ownership may form part of the application.

How much do I need for a down payment?

The amount depends on the purchase price, the property’s use and the mortgage program. How your income is documented can also affect the requirements. When planning your budget, allow for closing costs as well as the down payment.

Does applying affect my credit score?

A mortgage application can involve a hard credit check, which can affect your score. Your consent is required before a mortgage agent obtains your credit report. Checking your own credit report does not affect your score.

What documents will I need?

The list depends on your business and the mortgage program. Common documents include:

  • Personal tax returns, called T1 Generals, and Notices of Assessment, often for the past two years.
  • Recent business bank statements.
  • A business licence or articles of incorporation.
  • Business financial statements and, if incorporated, T2 corporate tax returns.
  • Signed contracts that support your income.
  • For a purchase, proof of your down payment and the purchase agreement.

The lender may request additional documents for your circumstances.
